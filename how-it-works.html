01 / THE SOUND

What are binaural beats?

A binaural beat is a perceived rhythm created when each ear receives a slightly different tone. For example, a 200 Hz tone in one ear and a 210 Hz tone in the other can produce the sensation of a 10 Hz beat. The beat is a listening phenomenon, rather than a separate 10 Hz sound played through the headphones. [1]

Hz means hertz: cycles per second. The pitch of the tones and the rate of the perceived beat are two different settings. In this example, 200 and 210 Hz describe the tones; 10 Hz describes their difference.

Stereo headphones keep the two tones separate, one for each ear. Ordinary speakers mix the channels in the room, so they do not preserve that same listening setup. [1]

02 / THE LABELS

What do brainwave frequencies mean?

Brainwaves are patterns of electrical activity recorded with equipment such as an EEG. Researchers group rhythms into frequency bands. The boundaries vary somewhat between studies; the names below are a useful introduction, not a set of separate mental modes. [2]

DeltaBelow about 4 Hz
ThetaAbout 4–8 Hz
AlphaAbout 8–13 Hz
BetaAbout 13–30 Hz
GammaAbove about 30 Hz

Seeing “alpha” or “theta” on an audio setting tells you about its frequency range. It does not tell you what your brain is doing. Alterstate does not measure brainwaves, and choosing a sound frequency is not the same as selecting a mental state.

A rhythm is not a promise.

“Brainwave entrainment” describes a proposed synchronization with rhythmic stimulation. A 2023 systematic review of 14 studies found inconsistent evidence for binaural-beat entrainment. That does not establish a reliable way to put someone into a particular state. [1]

Anxiety and Calmness: While direct brainwave entrainment is unproven, some randomized controlled trials show that listening to binaural beats can reduce subjective anxiety and lower blood pressure in specific settings.

03 / A QUIETER MOMENT

Alpha waves, relaxation, and encouraging research

Alpha activity is often observed during relaxed wakefulness with the eyes closed—one reason the term appears in relaxation audio. [3] Binaural-beat research also explores something more personal: how people feel after listening.

A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies reported beneficial effects across the outcomes examined, including anxiety. It offers an encouraging basis for continued research into binaural beats and the listening experience. [4]

In a small controlled study with 21 participants, listeners reported greater relaxation after exercise with binaural-beat audio than with similar audio without the binaural component. [5] The study used a theta-range recording, illustrating that relaxation research extends beyond alpha-labelled sounds.

Make room for a quieter moment.

In Alterstate, sound is an invitation to pause and turn your attention inward. Settle somewhere comfortable, choose a session that appeals to you, and give yourself time to listen.

04 / WINDING DOWN

Binaural beats for sleep: a gentler end to your day

A quieter room. Softer sound. A little time with nothing else to do. Alterstate’s sleep-themed sessions are designed to accompany that transition from a busy day to a restful evening.

Research on music combined with auditory beats offers encouraging findings about how people feel after listening. In a 2022 randomized trial, the combination produced greater reductions in cognitive state anxiety than music alone, beats alone, or pink noise among participants with moderate trait anxiety. [6] That result concerns short-term anxiety, rather than sleep itself, but is relevant to the interest in listening as a way to unwind.

Explore a sound you enjoy, keep the volume gentle, and let listening become part of your evening routine. There is no need to find a perfect frequency before you begin. These sessions are for winding down; they are not a treatment for insomnia.

A FEW MORE THINGS

Common questions

Can I use binaural beats during meditation?

Yes. You can make sound part of your meditation practice, using the listening experience as a focus for your attention. Choose a track you enjoy and an approach that feels comfortable.

What does research say about relaxation?

There are encouraging findings: a controlled study reported greater relaxation after listening to binaural beats, and a broader meta-analysis reported benefits including reduced anxiety. Studies use different recordings, listening conditions, and participant groups, so individual experiences can vary. [4] [5]

Where should I start with listening before bed?

Try a sleep-themed session that appeals to you, in a quiet space at a comfortable volume. Let your preferences guide you as you explore the available sounds.

Why combine binaural beats with other sounds?

Layers of sound shape the character of a session. Research has also explored combined listening: a 2026 randomized trial found greater reductions in anxiety and negative mood with music plus auditory beats than with pink noise. That study assessed the combination, rather than the contribution of beats alone. [7]

Does Alterstate use Apple Health to measure brainwaves?

No. Apple Health readings such as heart rate and HRV are different from EEG measurements. You can explore Alterstate’s audio experiences without measuring your brainwaves.
