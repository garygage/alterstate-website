01 / THE SOUND

What are binaural beats?

A binaural beat is a perceived rhythm created when each ear receives a slightly different tone. For example, a 200 Hz tone in one ear and a 210 Hz tone in the other can produce the sensation of a 10 Hz beat. The beat is a listening phenomenon, rather than a separate 10 Hz sound played through the headphones. [1]

Hz means hertz: cycles per second. The pitch of the tones and the rate of the perceived beat are two different settings. In this example, 200 and 210 Hz describe the tones; 10 Hz describes their difference.

Stereo headphones keep the two tones separate, one for each ear. Ordinary speakers mix the channels in the room, so they do not preserve that same listening setup. [1]

02 / THE LABELS

What do brainwave frequencies mean?

Brainwaves are patterns of electrical activity recorded with equipment such as an EEG. Researchers group rhythms into frequency bands. The boundaries vary somewhat between studies; the names below are a useful introduction, not a set of separate mental modes. [2]

DeltaBelow about 4 Hz
ThetaAbout 4–8 Hz
AlphaAbout 8–13 Hz
BetaAbout 13–30 Hz
GammaAbove about 30 Hz

Seeing “alpha” or “theta” on an audio setting tells you about its frequency range. It does not tell you what your brain is doing. Alterstate does not measure brainwaves, and choosing a sound frequency is not the same as selecting a mental state.

A rhythm is not a promise.

“Brainwave entrainment” describes a proposed synchronization with rhythmic stimulation. A 2023 systematic review of 14 studies found inconsistent evidence for binaural-beat entrainment. That does not establish a reliable way to put someone into a particular state. [1]

03 / A QUIETER MOMENT

Alpha waves for relaxation: what does that mean?

Alpha activity is often observed during relaxed wakefulness with the eyes closed. That association helps explain why alpha frequencies appear so often in descriptions of relaxation audio. It is an observation about brain activity—not proof that an alpha-labelled track will make you relaxed. [3]

Research on alpha and beta binaural beats has not consistently shown that the EEG follows the beat frequency. In one study, researchers found no such frequency-following effect. [4]

For your own listening, there is no need to chase a number. Choose a sound you find comfortable, keep the volume gentle, and notice whether you enjoy the experience. You can prefer a track without needing to claim that it changes your brainwaves.

04 / WINDING DOWN

Binaural beats for sleep: what can you expect?

A bedtime playlist may be part of the space you make for winding down. With binaural beats, however, a track’s frequency is not a guarantee that you will fall asleep, stay asleep, or enter a particular sleep stage.

Sleep itself is more varied than a single slow rhythm. It moves through non-REM and REM phases; during REM, recorded brain activity can resemble waking activity. A “delta” label on a recording is not evidence that it creates deep sleep. [5]

If you choose to listen before bed, pick a comfortable sound and a volume that feels unobtrusive. You do not need to keep listening if it is distracting or unpleasant. Alterstate’s sleep-themed sessions are listening experiences for winding down, not a treatment for insomnia or other sleep conditions.

A FEW MORE THINGS

Common questions

Are binaural beats the same as meditation?

No. Binaural beats describe an auditory effect; meditation is a practice. You can choose to use sound during meditation, but neither requires the other.

Does a higher frequency mean a stronger effect?

No such scale is established here. A higher beat rate means a faster rhythm, not a stronger or better result. Choose settings for comfort rather than intensity.

Is there one best binaural beat frequency for sleep?

This guide does not recommend a single frequency as a sleep solution. Frequency labels do not establish which sound will suit you or what sleep stage you will enter.

Does Alterstate use Apple Health to measure brainwaves?

No. Apple Health readings such as heart rate and HRV are not EEG measurements. Alterstate does not use them to measure or confirm a brainwave state.
